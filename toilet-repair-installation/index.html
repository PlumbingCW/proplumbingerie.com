When A Toilet Problem Is An Emergency

A toilet is a simple machine with a few parts that wear out. The fill valve, flapper and flush handle control the tank. The wax ring and closet bolts seal the base to the drain. A clog or a failing flange causes most of the larger problems.

When The Floor Is Involved

A toilet that rocks or leaks for weeks can rot the subfloor around the flange. We check the floor when we pull a toilet for repair. If the flange is cracked or sits below the floor, we fix it before the toilet goes back.

A toilet that keeps clogging may have a problem in the drain, not the bowl. A camera inspection checks that, and drain cleaning clears what the plunger cannot.

Repair Or Replace

A toilet with a cracked bowl or tank should be replaced. A toilet over twenty years old that uses a lot of water per flush is worth replacing if you are already paying for repairs. A newer toilet usually costs less to run and flushes more reliably. Same-day repair appointments are available when the schedule allows, and a flooding toilet goes to the 24/7 line after hours.

Low-Flow Toilets And Flush Performance

Modern toilets use less water per flush than older models, and a well-designed bowl still clears waste reliably. A toilet that flushes weakly often has clogged rim jets, a low tank water level or a partial clog in the trap, not a design flaw. The plumber checks these before blaming the fixture.

If you are replacing, pick a toilet with a full-size trapway and a flush rating you can verify. Ask about a toilet with a tank lever you can reach easily, and a seat height that suits the household.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and a flooding toilet is handled through the 24/7 line at any hour.

A toilet has to be anchored to the floor. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for water closets to be set on a floor flange that is securely fastened, which is why a rocking toilet is a code problem and not only an annoyance. The plumber checks the flange and the bolts whenever a toilet is reset.

Installing A New Toilet

A new toilet needs the right rough-in distance, usually twelve inches from the wall to the flange bolts, though older homes vary. Measure before you buy, or let the plumber measure. A comfort-height bowl, an elongated seat and a quiet fill valve are common choices.

If you are remodeling, a new toilet goes with other work. See plumbing installation for new bathrooms and faucet repair for sink fixtures. We serve Lafayette too on the Lafayette page.
