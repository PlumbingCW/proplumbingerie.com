Where Vista Ridge Is

Vista Ridge is a neighborhood on the southern edge of the Town of Erie, in Weld County, ZIP 80516. The Colorado National Golf Club, an 18-hole course at 2700 Vista Parkway, is the community's anchor, and fairways run between residential streets. A Vista Ridge Homeowners Association is listed for the community.

BizWest reported in 2002 that Vista Ridge Development Corp. was building the community around the course, which was then under construction. That puts the start of development in the early 2000s.

Homes And Housing Types

Homes.com lists about 1,958 homes in Vista Ridge with a median year built of 2005. The mix includes single-family homes, townhouses and condos. These are listing-site figures rather than an official count, but they match the picture of a community built mostly in the 2000s.

What Wears Out In Homes Of This Age

This section is general plumbing information, not a claim about any particular house. In any home that has been lived in for roughly two decades, the parts that move or hold heat wear out on their own schedules. Tank water heaters, toilet fill valves, faucet cartridges, supply shutoff valves and garbage disposals are the usual ones.

Replacing a worn part before it fails is often cheaper than dealing with the water damage after. If your water heater is old, noisy or has started to leak around the base, have it looked at. See water heater replacement for what the decision usually turns on.

Water heaters

Lukewarm water, rumbling or a damp floor near the tank. See repair and replacement.

Running or leaking toilets

A toilet that keeps filling often needs a new fill valve or flapper. See toilet repair.

Dripping faucets

A drip at the spout or handle usually points to a worn cartridge or seat. See faucet repair.

Kitchen disposals

A disposal that hums, jams or leaks under the sink. See disposal repair.

Townhomes And Condos: Check Your HOA First

Because Vista Ridge includes attached homes, a leak can affect a neighbor. The association's governing documents usually say who is responsible for what, including the pipes inside the walls and the building's shutoff. Read them or ask your management contact before you hire anyone for a shared-wall problem.

Checking For A Hidden Leak

The Town of Erie has been installing smart water meters, and customers can watch usage through the EyeOnWater or WaterScope tools. A sudden jump in use with nobody home is a good reason to look for a leak. Turn off every tap and appliance, then check whether the meter is still moving.

If it is, the leak is somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. A plumber can trace it with leak detection equipment instead of opening walls at random.
