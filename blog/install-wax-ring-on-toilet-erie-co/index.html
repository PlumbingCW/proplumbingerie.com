How to Install a Wax Ring on a Toilet in Erie, CO

Water at the base of a toilet, a toilet that rocks or a sewage smell in the bathroom usually points to a failed wax ring. The seal is cheap, and replacing it is a job many homeowners can do with an open afternoon and a strong back.

This guide walks through the steps, the mistakes that cause repeat leaks and the signs that a plumber should take over.

What The Wax Ring Does

The wax ring sits between the toilet horn and the floor flange. It seals the joint so wastewater goes into the drain and sewer gas stays out. If the toilet moves, the flange sits too low or the wax is old, the seal can fail.

Signs the seal has failed

  • Water on the floor at the base after flushing
  • A toilet that rocks or shifts when you sit
  • A sewer smell that returns after cleaning
  • A stained or soft spot in the floor near the toilet
  • A toilet that was removed and reset recently without a new ring

What You Need

A new wax ring or a wax-free seal, new closet bolts, a putty knife, an adjustable wrench, rags, a bucket and a sponge. Have a helper for lifting, since a toilet is heavy and the porcelain chips easily. Buy a ring sized for the flange height, with a horn if the flange sits flush.

Step By Step

1

Shut off and drain

Close the supply valve, flush and sponge out the remaining water. Disconnect the supply line.

2

Remove the toilet

Remove the nuts at the base and lift straight up. Set it on towels, not on its side.

3

Clean the flange

Scrape off the old wax, check the flange for cracks and stuff a rag in the drain to block gas.

4

Set the new seal

Place new bolts, put the ring on the flange or the horn, then lower the toilet straight down and press.

5

Tighten and test

Snug the nuts a little at a time, reconnect the supply and flush several times while watching the base.

Do not twist the toilet after it touches the wax. The seal squashes unevenly and leaks. Lower it straight and press once.

Where The Job Goes Wrong

A flange that sits below the finished floor is the most common cause of repeat leaks. If the flange is lower than the floor, a standard ring may not seal. A thicker ring or a flange spacer fixes it, but a cracked or rusted flange needs repair.

Over-tightening bolts cracks the porcelain. Under-tightening lets the toilet move. Alternate between nuts and stop when the toilet no longer rocks.

When To Call A Plumber

Call if the flange is broken, the floor is soft, the toilet keeps leaking after a new ring or sewage is backing up. A soft floor means water has been leaking for a while. Fixing the seal without repairing the floor will not last.

If the toilet clogs often, the drain may be the problem, and drain cleaning clears what a plunger cannot. We also serve Lafayette, as described on the Lafayette page, and other towns on the service area page.

Tips For A Cleaner Install

Dry-fit the toilet over the flange before you place the ring, and mark the position of the bolts. Check that the toilet sits level and that the bowl is square to the wall. A few minutes of preparation prevents lifting the toilet twice.

Keep the wax at room temperature. A cold ring is stiff and seals poorly. If your bathroom is chilly, warm the ring in a pocket or a warm room for a short time before you use it.

Checking The Subfloor

When the toilet is off, press on the floor around the flange. Soft or dark wood means a long-term leak and rot. A floor that gives under pressure cannot hold a toilet steady, and the new seal will fail again. Repair the floor before you reset the toilet.

Erie Notes: Soil Movement And A Rocking Toilet

The Colorado Geological Survey publishes a swelling soils guide for Colorado homebuyers and homeowners. It describes clay soils that expand when wet and can move floors and foundations.

If a toilet starts rocking again after a careful reset, floor movement may be part of the cause. Mention it to the plumber so the flange and the floor are checked together.
