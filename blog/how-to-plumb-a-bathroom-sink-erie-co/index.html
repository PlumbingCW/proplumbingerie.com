How to Plumb a Bathroom Sink: An Erie, CO Homeowner Guide

A bathroom sink has three plumbing systems packed into one cabinet. Hot and cold water come in through supply lines, used water leaves through the drain and trap, and a vent lets air in behind the water so it flows smoothly. Understanding all three makes the job far less mysterious.

This guide covers a replacement or a new sink on existing rough-in lines, plus the points where a plumber is the safer choice.

The Three Parts Of A Sink Hookup

Supply

Hot and cold lines run from shutoff valves, called angle stops, to the faucet through short flexible lines.

Drain and trap

The tailpiece drops from the sink into a P-trap, which holds water to block sewer gas, then connects to the wall drain.

Vent

A pipe in the wall carries air so the trap does not siphon dry. It is hidden but essential.

Most replacement jobs touch only the faucet, the supply lines and the trap. The wall pipes stay put. If the wall pipes are in the wrong place, the job changes from a swap to a plumbing alteration.

Order Of Work For A Replacement

1

Shut off and open

Close both angle stops. Open the faucet to release pressure. Place a bucket under the trap.

2

Disconnect

Loosen the trap nuts and the supply line nuts. Remove the old sink or faucet.

3

Install the faucet

Mount the faucet on the sink before the sink goes down, which is easier than reaching under a cabinet.

4

Set the sink and drain

Seal the drain flange with plumber's putty or the gasket the maker supplies.

5

Connect supply and trap

Use new supply lines. Align the trap so the weight does not pull on the joints.

6

Test

Open the stops slowly, run water and look at every joint with a dry paper towel.

Hot is usually on the left and cold on the right when you face the faucet. If they are reversed, the faucet will deliver water at the wrong handle. Swap the supply lines before you close the cabinet.

When To Hire A Plumber

If the shutoff valves are stuck or leak when turned, replace them first. If the wall drain is corroded, if the sink is moving to a new wall or if you need a new vent, the job is no longer a simple swap. New drain and vent lines also need to meet code and may need a permit.

A sink that drains slowly after a new install may have a clog downstream, which is where drain cleaning comes in. Larger bathroom work belongs to plumbing installation. Homeowners in Lafayette can read how we cover that city on our Lafayette page, and the service area page lists the other towns.

Choosing Compatible Parts

Faucet, sink and drain parts need to match. A three-hole sink takes a widespread or centerset faucet, while a single-hole sink takes a single-handle faucet. The drain assembly differs for sinks with or without an overflow hole, so check the box before you leave the store.

Pop-up drains use a lift rod that moves a stopper through the tailpiece. If the stopper does not seal, the rod is usually out of adjustment. A few turns of the nut on the pivot strap often fix it.

Checking Your Work

After the first test, wait an hour and check again with a dry paper towel at each joint. Slow leaks show up as damp spots on the towel. Tighten only what leaks, and a little at a time.

The rules behind this hookup are in the plumbing code. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires every fixture to have a trap and a vent, and it limits how far a trap can sit from its vent. That is why moving a sink is a plumbing alteration, not a cosmetic change.

Erie Notes: Permits And Licensing

The Town of Erie Building Division handles permits and inspections at 303-926-2780, and permit applications go through the Town's online permit portal. Replacing a faucet on existing lines is different from adding a drain or vent, so ask the Building Division what applies to your project before you start.

If you hire out the work, you can check a plumber's license through the Colorado State Plumbing Board's licensee lookup on the Division of Professions and Occupations site.

A Short Tool List

Gather these before you start

  • Adjustable wrench and channel-lock pliers
  • Basin wrench for faucet nuts in tight spaces
  • New braided supply lines of the right length
  • Plumber's putty or silicone for the drain flange
  • Bucket, rags and a flashlight
