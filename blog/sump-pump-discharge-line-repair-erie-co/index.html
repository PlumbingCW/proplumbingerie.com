Sump Pump Discharge Line Repair for Erie, CO Homes

A sump pump pushes groundwater out of the pit, but the water still has to go somewhere. The discharge line carries it away from the house. If that line freezes, clogs, cracks or dumps water next to the foundation, the pump runs and the problem returns.

This guide covers the common discharge line failures, how to fix them and what to ask about where the water goes.

How A Discharge Line Fails

Discharge too closeWater returns to the foundationExtend the line farther away

A pump that cycles every few seconds often has a check valve problem. Water drains back into the pit after each cycle, so the pump has to repeat the work. Replace the valve before you replace the pump.

Where Should The Water Go?

The goal is to move water well away from the foundation, onto ground that slopes away. A discharge that spills next to the wall just recycles the water. Many people extend the line to a lower part of the yard or a drainage swale.

Do not discharge to a neighbor's property. Do not send it onto a sidewalk that will ice. Most places do not allow connecting a sump discharge to the sanitary sewer, because storm water overloads sewers. Ask the Town before you plan a connection.

Winter Problems

Preventing a frozen discharge

  • Keep the outlet clear of snow and ice
  • Use a larger-diameter pipe near the end so ice does not block it
  • Slope the line so water drains out after each cycle
  • Install a freeze-resistant outlet or a bypass that opens if the end freezes
  • Check the line after the first hard freeze

A frozen outlet makes the pump push against a solid block. It can overheat and burn out. Shut off the pump if you suspect a freeze and call a plumber before the pit overflows.

Choosing Pipe And Fittings

Discharge lines are usually PVC or flexible corrugated pipe. Rigid PVC with glued joints is more durable and holds slope better. Flexible pipe is easy to install but can collect water and freeze in low spots.

Use the size the pump requires, commonly one and a half or two inches. Using a smaller pipe restricts flow and makes the pump work harder. Use proper couplings and cement rather than tape or clamps.

Grading And Outlet Design

A discharge line should slope away from the house so water drains out after each cycle. The outlet should sit above ground level and away from walkways. A splash block or a short pop-up emitter spreads the flow.

Avoid ending the line where water can pool against a neighbor's fence or a driveway. Water that returns to the foundation defeats the purpose of the pump.

Testing After A Repair

Pour water into the pit until the pump starts. Walk the line and watch for leaks and for flow at the outlet. Listen for the check valve closing. A quiet run with water leaving the outlet means the repair worked.

The plumbing code is firm on where this water may go. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) says storm water may not be drained into sewers intended for sewage only, which is why a sump discharge belongs outdoors. Your local authority can confirm its own rules.

Erie Notes: Weather, Soil And Where Water Should Go

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals put Erie's annual precipitation at 16.17 inches. That is modest, but spring snowmelt and short intense storms deliver water in bursts, which is when pumps and discharge lines get tested.

The Colorado Geological Survey's swelling soils guide says keeping water away from foundations is a main defense in clay soils. A discharge line that ends several feet from the wall, on ground that slopes away, supports that goal.
