Outdoor Faucet Leak Repair: An Erie, CO Homeowner Guide

An outdoor faucet that drips all summer wastes water, and one that leaks inside the wall can ruin drywall without showing it. The right fix depends on where the water appears, so start by watching.

This guide covers the common leaks, how to repair them and what to do before the first freeze.

A hose bib leaking at the handle while running is often a packing problem. A hose bib that leaks only after cold weather is a warning sign of a split pipe behind the wall.

Fixing A Dripping Spout

1

Shut off the water

Close the interior valve for the line if there is one, or the main shutoff.

2

Open and remove the handle

Take off the handle screw and handle, then loosen the packing nut.

3

Remove the stem

Unscrew the valve stem and look at the rubber washer at its tip.

4

Replace and reassemble

Install a matching washer or a new stem, then reassemble and test.

A frost-free hose bib has a long stem and a seal that sits inside the heated part of the house. If the stem is bent or the seal is worn, the whole assembly may need replacing.

Before The First Freeze

Winter steps for outdoor faucets

  • Disconnect every hose, splitter and timer
  • Close the interior shutoff to the line, if one exists
  • Open the outdoor faucet to drain what remains
  • Install an insulated faucet cover on any non-frost-free bib
  • Replace old non-frost-free faucets with frost-free models

A hose left attached traps water in the faucet. When it freezes, it expands and splits the pipe behind the wall. You find out in spring when you turn the water on.

When The Leak Is Inside

If you see staining on a basement ceiling near the faucet, assume the pipe has split. Shut off the water to the line and call a plumber. Our burst pipe repair page covers what happens next, and leak detection helps when you cannot see the source.

Our faucet repair page covers interior and outdoor fixtures. We also dispatch to nearby cities, as described on the Lafayette page and the service area page.

Choosing A Frost-Free Hose Bib

A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that places the seat well inside the wall, where it stays warm. When you shut the faucet, water drains out of the pipe if the faucet slopes slightly downward. Choose the stem length to match the wall thickness.

A frost-free bib still freezes if a hose is left on, because water cannot drain. That is why disconnecting hoses matters even with a frost-free model.

Hoses And Vacuum Breakers

A vacuum breaker on a hose bib prevents water in a hose from flowing back into the house supply. It may drip a little when the faucet is running, which is normal. If it leaks when the faucet is off, replace it.

Never submerge a hose end in a bucket, pool or chemical sprayer without a backflow device. Contaminated water can be pulled back into household pipes.

Spotting A Slow Leak

A slow leak at the wall may not show outdoors. Check the interior side of the wall in a basement or crawlspace for damp insulation, stains or a musty smell. A moisture meter helps, and so does a flashlight.

Hose connections are covered by the plumbing code. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for backflow protection on hose bibs, which is why most have a vacuum breaker. Replace a breaker that leaks when the faucet is off, and never remove it to stop a drip.

Erie Notes: Smart Meters And Leak Alerts

The Town of Erie reports that more than 60 percent of its water meters are smart meters, and customers can get leak alerts by email or text through EyeOnWater and WaterScope. A running outdoor faucet is a common cause of those alerts.

After you repair a leak, the Town's Utility Billing office at 303-926-2700 can explain its leak adjustment process. Keep your repair receipt and the dates of the leak.
