Kitchen Plumbing Cost for Erie, CO Homeowners

Kitchen plumbing sits behind cabinets, so it only gets attention when something leaks or a remodel opens the walls. The cost depends on how much changes, from swapping fixtures in place to moving the layout.

This guide explains the parts of a kitchen plumbing bill so you can plan a budget and compare bids.

In-Place Swap Versus Layout Change

Replacing a faucet, a sink or a disposal in the same location is a quick job. Moving the sink to another wall, adding an island sink or relocating the dishwasher means new supply, drain and vent lines. That is where the cost climbs.

Hidden Costs Behind The Cabinets

Old shutoff valves often fail when turned, and old supply lines may be brittle. Plumbers usually recommend replacing them during a remodel, since access is open. It adds a small cost now and avoids a larger repair later.

Drain lines matter too. If the kitchen drain has slow flow or is made of old galvanized or cast iron, replacing it while the walls are open costs less than doing it later. A camera inspection of the main can show whether the line past the kitchen is sound.

Fixtures Versus Labor

Fixtures vary widely in price, from basic faucets to commercial-style models with extra hoses and fittings. A pot filler or an instant hot tap adds supply lines and sometimes electrical. Ask the plumber to list fixtures and labor separately so you can see what is a choice and what is required.

Planning The Sink Area

The sink is the center of kitchen plumbing. Its drain is usually two inches, and it needs a trap and a vent. Moving the sink more than a few feet may require a new vent path, and an island sink usually needs a loop vent or an air admittance valve if local code allows.

Think about the cabinet too. A deep apron sink or a heavy cast iron sink changes the base cabinet and the drain position. Choose the sink first and give the plumber the specifications.

Gas And Appliance Lines

A gas range needs a gas line with a shutoff valve and a flexible connector. A new line or a longer run is gas piping that needs a permit and a pressure test. If the range is moving, ask the plumber to check the line size.

Refrigerator ice makers and pot fillers need small supply lines with shutoffs. Plan them when walls are open, since adding them later means cutting finished surfaces.

Budgeting Without Surprises

Items that surprise first-time remodelers

  • Old shutoff valves that must be replaced
  • Corroded drain lines found behind cabinets
  • Electrical needs for a disposal or an instant hot tap
  • Permit fees and inspection scheduling
  • Patching and finishing after the rough-in

Ask the plumber for a written scope that separates fixtures, labor and permits. Add a reserve for what is found behind the walls.

Venting an island sink is a code decision. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) allows air admittance valves in some cases, while many local authorities restrict or prohibit them, so the plumber confirms what the local office accepts before pricing the work.

Erie Notes: Permits, Licenses And Home Age

Erie grew from 6,291 residents in 2000 to 30,038 in 2020, so many kitchens are in homes built in recent decades. Those kitchens often have plastic drains and builder-grade fixtures that are easy to swap. Older Old Town kitchens may have older metal lines that deserve a closer look.

The Town Building Division at 303-926-2780 handles permits and inspections. Colorado licenses plumbers through the State Plumbing Board, and you can use the licensee lookup to check anyone you hire.
