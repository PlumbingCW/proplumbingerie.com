Tankless Water Heater Federal Tax Credit: Erie, CO Guide

Search results still show many pages promising a federal tax credit for tankless water heaters. Some are outdated. The IRS says the Section 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit ends for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.

This guide explains what that means for an Erie homeowner in late 2026, what records to keep and where to look for other help. We are plumbers, not tax advisors, so treat this as background and confirm with a tax professional.

What The IRS Says

The IRS energy credit FAQ states that the credit will not be allowed for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025. You can read the agency's own energy credit FAQs. That means a tankless heater installed in 2026 is not eligible for the federal 25C credit.

Property is considered placed in service when it is ready and available for use, not when you order it or pay a deposit. A unit installed and working in December 2025 is treated differently from one installed in January 2026. A tax professional can tell you how that applies to your return.

How To Treat Old Articles

Check the date on any page that promises a credit. Articles written before the law changed may still rank in search. If a page does not name a year or cite the IRS, treat its claim with caution. When in doubt, go to the IRS site.

Manufacturers and contractors may also advertise incentives. Ask what the incentive is, who pays it and what paperwork you need. A claim that sounds too good probably has conditions.

Other Places To Look For Help

Questions to ask about incentives

  • Does my electric or gas utility offer a rebate for the model I am considering
  • Does the manufacturer have a current promotion, and what are the conditions
  • Does the state or county have a program for efficient water heaters
  • What documents do I need, such as an invoice, model number and permit record
  • Who is responsible for filing, me or the installer

We do not list specific utility programs here because they change and we have not verified any for this page. Ask your utility directly. Keep every invoice, the model and serial number and the permit record, since programs often require them.

Choosing On The Merits

Without a federal credit, the decision rests on how a tankless unit fits your home. Consider hot water demand, available gas supply, venting and long-term maintenance. Our tankless water heater page explains what the install needs.

If you decide to stay with a tank, see water heater replacement. We also dispatch to nearby cities. See our Lafayette page and the service area page.

Keeping Good Records

Whether or not a credit applies, keep paperwork for any major home improvement. The invoice, the model and serial numbers, the permit and the inspection record are useful for insurance, warranty and resale. Store copies digitally and on paper.

Take photos of the install, the data label and the vent and gas connections. If a question arises years later, photos help a new plumber or an inspector understand what was done.

Talking To A Tax Professional

Tax rules depend on your situation. A tax professional can tell you whether any credit applies to a 2025 install, whether other improvements count and how to document them. Bring your invoice and dates.

Be wary of anyone who promises a refund based on a purchase. Credits depend on your own return, and only a qualified person who knows your finances can advise you.

Thinking About Total Ownership Cost

Compare water heaters by the cost of the unit, the install, the energy use, the maintenance and the expected life. A lower purchase price can hide higher running costs, and a higher price can pay back through lower bills. Ask the plumber for estimates on the same terms for each option.

According to the IRS, the credit is part of a group of home energy provisions that were terminated early, so a promise of a refund on a 2026 install should raise a question. Ask the seller to point to the IRS guidance in writing.

Erie Notes: Permits And Paper Trail

A December 4, 2025 bulletin from the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies says water heater installs and replacements require a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor, with permits and inspections from the local authority. Read the bulletin. A permitted job leaves a paper trail that helps with any rebate or insurance question.

In Erie, the Building Division at 303-926-2780 handles permits and inspections. Ask the plumber to give you the permit number and the final inspection date along with your invoice.
