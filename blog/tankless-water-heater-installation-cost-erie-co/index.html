Tankless Water Heater Installation Cost in Erie, CO

People searching for tankless installation cost usually want one number. A fair answer is that the price of the heater is the smaller part of the project. The pipes, vents and power that feed it decide most of the total, and those differ house to house.

This guide explains what goes into a tankless quote so you can compare bids on the same terms.

Why There Is No Single Number

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it needs a lot of energy at once. A gas model needs a larger gas supply and a sealed vent, while an electric model needs a heavy circuit. If your house already has those, the job is simpler, and if not, the upgrade is the cost.

Two homes with the same heater can get very different quotes for that reason. A quote that only lists the unit is incomplete. A good quote lists every item that supports it.

Sizing: Flow And Temperature Rise

A tankless heater is rated by how much water it heats per minute and how much it can raise the temperature. Winter incoming water is colder than summer water, so a unit sized for summer may struggle in January. The plumber should size for the coldest month, not the average.

Count the fixtures that might run together, such as a shower and a dishwasher. If two showers could run at once, the unit has to handle both. An undersized unit is the most common reason homeowners are disappointed with tankless.

Costs After Installation

Tankless units need a descaling flush each year, and scale buildup varies with the water. A softener or filter ahead of the unit can reduce scale, which is covered in our water softener and filtration page. Ask whether the install includes service valves, since they make flushing simple.

Compare the whole project, not only the heater. Our tankless water heater page explains what we install and how we service units. If you are weighing a tank instead, see water heater replacement.

The Federal Tax Credit Has Changed

The IRS says the Section 25C home energy credit does not apply to property placed in service after December 31, 2025, according to its energy credit FAQs. If you read an older article that promises a federal credit for tankless installs, check the date. We are not tax advisors, so confirm anything with a tax professional.

Tank Versus Tankless: A Fair Comparison

A tank heater is simpler and usually cheaper to install, because it uses the connections already in most homes. A tankless unit costs more up front, takes less space and avoids standby heat loss. The right choice depends on how long you plan to stay and how your household uses hot water.

A tank can fail by leaking and flooding. A tankless unit has no tank to burst, but it has more parts that need service, such as sensors and heat exchangers. Neither is maintenance free, so compare the upkeep along with the price.

Questions To Ask Every Bidder

Get these answers in writing

  • Which model and capacity are you recommending, and how did you size it
  • Is the gas line, meter or electrical panel adequate, and if not, what is the upgrade
  • Where will the vent exit the house and what material will you use
  • Where does the condensate drain and is a neutralizer included
  • Who pulls the permit and schedules the inspection
  • Are service valves included for yearly flushing

If one bid leaves out a major item, the lower number is not a better deal. Ask each plumber to list the same items so the totals can be compared.

Erie Notes: License And Permit Requirements

Colorado's State Plumbing Board says water heater installs and replacements require a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor, with permits and inspections from the local authority. The Board sent that notice on December 4, 2025, and you can read it in the DORA bulletin.

In Erie, the Building Division at 303-926-2780 handles permits. A tankless install usually touches the gas line and the vent, which are both inspection items, so ask any bidder who pulls the permit.
