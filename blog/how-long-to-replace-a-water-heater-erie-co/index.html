How Long Does It Take to Replace a Water Heater in Erie, CO?

Most homeowners who ask how long a water heater replacement takes are standing in a cold shower or a wet utility room. The honest answer is that a straight swap of one tank for another is a matter of hours, while a fuel change or a new venting path can take a full day or more.

This guide breaks the job into parts so you can see where the time goes and what you can do to keep it short.

The Short Answer By Job Type

Time depends less on the heater and more on what surrounds it. A new tank that goes where the old one stood, with the same fuel and the same vent, is the fastest case. Anything that changes the connections adds work.

Typical replacement timelines
JobTypical working timeWhat extends it
Same-fuel tank swap, same locationA few hoursCorroded shutoffs, stuck fittings, tight access
Gas tank with a new ventHalf a day to a full dayVent routing, wall or roof penetrations
Electric to gas or the reverseA day or moreNew gas line or new circuit, inspection scheduling
Tank to tanklessA day or moreGas line upsizing, sealed venting, condensate drain
Heat pump (hybrid) tankSeveral hours to a dayCondensate drain, clearance, electrical

These are ranges for the hands-on work, not a promise for any single house. The plumber can give a more specific estimate after seeing the space, and the price is quoted before work begins.

Where The Time Goes During A Swap

1

Prep and shutoff

The plumber turns off the water, gas or power and protects the floor. This is quick if the valves work.

2

Drain and remove

The old tank drains, often through a hose to a floor drain or outside. A full tank takes a while to empty.

3

Set, connect, vent

The new unit is placed, connected to water and fuel and vented. This is the longest step.

4

Fill, heat and test

The tank fills, burners or elements fire and the plumber checks for leaks and safe operation.

After the plumber leaves, the tank still needs time to heat. A gas tank often recovers faster than an electric one, and a large tank takes longer than a small one. Plan on a wait before the first hot shower.

What Can Slow A Replacement Down

Common delays

  • Corroded shutoff valves or unions that break when turned
  • A gas line or vent that does not meet current code
  • A new unit that does not fit the space and needs a different model
  • Permit and inspection timing, which depends on the local office
  • Parts that need to be sourced for an unusual size or fuel

Most of these can be spotted in advance. Photograph the unit, the label and the surrounding space and share them when you call. If you can say the fuel, the capacity and where the vent exits, the plumber can arrive with the right parts.

Keeping Things Moving While You Wait

Shut off the cold supply valve above the tank and switch off the gas control or the breaker. If the tank is leaking, connect a hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain to limit damage. Clear a path to the heater so the crew can work without moving boxes.

If you are choosing between repair and replacement, read about water heater repair first. If you already know the tank has failed, see water heater replacement. We also dispatch to nearby cities, as described on our Lafayette page and the service area page.

Gas Versus Electric: Why Recovery Differs

A gas tank heats water with a burner under the tank, so it recovers quickly after a big draw. An electric tank uses submerged elements that heat more slowly. That difference matters on the day of the install, since the shower you want may be an hour or two away on an electric model.

Hybrid heat pump tanks recover more slowly still when they run in efficiency mode. Many have a boost setting that uses extra heat. Ask the plumber how to switch modes if you need hot water sooner.

Scheduling Tips That Save Time

Clear the area around the heater, move vehicles from the driveway if the crew needs access and have the water heater label photo ready. Tell the plumber about low ceilings, tight stairs or locked doors in advance. A prepared house shortens the visit more than any tool does.

Code adds a few steps that take time. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, a drain pan where a leak could cause damage and expansion control on closed systems. A plumber who installs all three is not padding the job, and an inspector will look for them.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the cold supply valve above the tank and switch off the gas control or the breaker
  • If the tank is leaking, run a hose from the drain valve to a floor drain
  • Keep children and pets away from hot water and from standing water near outlets
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber
  • Clear a path to the heater and photograph its data label

Erie Notes: Who Can Do The Work And Who Signs Off

A bulletin sent on December 4, 2025 by the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies covers who may do this work. It says the State Plumbing Board requires a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor, along with permits and inspections from the authority having jurisdiction. You can read the bulletin yourself.

In Erie, permits and inspections run through the Town's Building Division at 645 Holbrook Street, reachable at 303-926-2780. Because the inspection is part of the job, timing on that end can affect when a project is fully closed out.
