What Drives Whole House Repipe Cost in Erie, CO

Repiping a house is a big project, and a quote can look very different from one plumber to the next. The reason is that the price depends on what is behind your walls and how the plumber plans to reach it.

This guide lists the cost drivers so you can compare bids on equal terms.

Why A Repipe Is Priced By The House

A repipe replaces the supply lines that bring water to fixtures. A small one-bath home has a short run. A large two-story house with four bathrooms has far more pipe, fittings and openings. Each fixture adds time.

Access matters just as much. Pipes in an open basement or crawlspace are quick to replace. Pipes in finished walls, under slabs or in tight attics take longer and require more patching afterward.

Copper, PEX Or CPVC

Copper has a long record and handles heat. PEX is flexible and fast to install, with fewer joints. CPVC is rigid and lower in cost, though it can become brittle with age. A good plumber explains the pick in terms of your house, not only price.

Ask how the plumber handles joints and supports. Hangers, sleeves where pipes pass through framing and proper expansion room all affect how long the new system lasts.

Signs You Are Past Patching

When repiping makes sense

  • Leaks at more than one location in a few years
  • Rusty or discolored water at several taps
  • Low pressure that valve work does not fix
  • Galvanized steel or polybutylene pipe
  • Pinhole leaks in copper that keep appearing

If you only have a single leak, the cost of leak detection and a spot repair is a smaller step. Our repiping page explains the process and how we plan a walk-through. If the water heater is old, ask about replacement at the same time.

How To Compare Bids

Ask each plumber for a written scope that names the pipe material, the number of fixtures, the access plan, the permit and what is left for other trades. A lower price that leaves out drywall and the permit is not lower. Ask how the plumber handles surprises once walls are opened.

Your Water Heater And Fixtures

A repipe connects to the water heater, so the connections are replaced too. If the heater is old, ask whether replacing it during the repipe saves a second visit. Fixture shutoffs also get new valves, which is worth doing with the walls open.

Old galvanized stubs at fixtures can be hard to remove and may add time. A good bid says how these are handled.

Planning Around Drywall And Paint

Plumbers open small sections of drywall to run new pipe. How much depends on the layout and the pipe material. PEX bends around corners and may need fewer openings than rigid pipe.

Decide ahead of time who patches the walls and when. If you plan to repaint rooms anyway, a repipe can fit the same schedule, and one crew can finish the surfaces.

Insurance, Warranties And Records

Ask what warranty comes with the pipe, the fittings and the labor, and get it in writing. Keep the invoice, the permit and the inspection record with your other house documents. They help with a future sale and with any insurance question about water damage.

Living In The House During The Work

Expect short periods without water as sections are cut over. Fill buckets for toilets, keep bottled water on hand and arrange showers elsewhere if the plan calls for it. A clear schedule from the plumber makes this easier.

Protect floors, pets and anything stored near the walls the crew will open. A tidy site reduces dust and speeds the work.

Materials must be code-approved. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) lists the pipe and fitting types allowed for potable water, and local authorities adopt and amend that list. A bid that names an approved material is easier to compare than one that says only new pipe.

What to do before the crew arrives

  • Clear the areas around fixtures, the water heater and the main shutoff
  • Move furniture and valuables away from walls that will be opened
  • Fill containers with drinking water and plan where to use a bathroom while water is off
  • Keep children and pets out of the work area
  • Photograph the walls and floors before work starts

Erie Notes: Lead Rules And Permits

Colorado banned lead pipe in 1988, and the Town of Erie says it has no town-owned lead service lines. The Town owns the service line from the main through the meter pit plus five feet past the meter, and the homeowner owns the rest.

Permits and inspections for a repipe go through the Town Building Division at 303-926-2780. Ask any bidder to include the permit in the written scope.
