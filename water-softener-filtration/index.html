Water Softener and Filtration in Erie, CO

Spots on glasses, scale on fixtures and a film on shower doors are signs of mineral content in the water. A softener reduces scale. A filter improves taste or removes specific contaminants. They are not the same thing, and the right choice depends on what is actually in your water.

We do not guess at hardness. Test your tap water first, and the plumber can help you read the result. Scale inside a tank is a common reason to act, as covered under water heater repair.

Signs Your Water May Need Treatment

Common signs

  • White crust on faucets and showerheads
  • Spotted glassware that does not wash clean
  • Reduced hot water flow or scale in the water heater
  • Dry skin or stiff laundry after washing
  • Chlorine taste or smell from the tap

Protecting Water Heaters And Fixtures

Scale insulates the bottom of a tank heater and coats the heat exchanger in a tankless unit. Treated water means less scale, longer fixture life and fewer repairs. It is a good partner to water heater installation and replacement.

A softener changes the feel of water. Some people dislike the slick feel, and those on a sodium-restricted diet should ask a doctor about drinking softened water. A separate drinking water tap can bypass the softener.

Look for certification on any equipment you buy. NSF/ANSI Standard 44 covers cation exchange water softeners, Standard 42 covers filters for chlorine taste and odor and Standard 53 covers filters that reduce health-related contaminants. A label that cites the standard is more reliable than one that only says improves water.
