Whole House Repiping in Erie, CO

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a house when repairs have stopped working. Pinhole leaks, rust-colored water, low pressure and repeated patching are the signs. A new system of copper or PEX ends the cycle of small repairs and the damage they cause.

A repipe is a planned project, not an emergency, unless a pipe has burst. For a burst pipe, see burst pipe repair.

Signs Your Pipes Are Failing

Common reasons to repipe

  • Pinhole leaks that appear in more than one place
  • Brown or rusty water, especially after the water has been off
  • Low water pressure across the house that a valve repair does not fix
  • Visible green corrosion or white mineral crusting at joints
  • Galvanized or polybutylene pipe, both of which have failure histories

Galvanized steel rusts from the inside and narrows over time. Polybutylene was widely used in the 1980s and early 1990s and has a record of failures at fittings. Copper can develop pinholes from the water or from flux and installation problems.

The right choice depends on the house, your budget and the plumber's recommendation. Most repipes today use PEX or copper. Ask for the reasons behind the plumber's pick.

Lead And Old Service Lines

Colorado banned lead pipe in 1988, and the Town of Erie says it has no town-owned lead service lines. Older homes elsewhere may still have lead solder or old fittings. If you are unsure what material your plumbing uses, a plumber can identify it during the site visit.

The Town owns the service line through the meter pit plus five feet beyond the meter. Everything after that is yours. A full repipe covers your side and can include the service line to the house. See water service line repair.

How A Repipe Is Done

1

Walk-through

The plumber maps the supply lines and fixtures and decides how to route new pipe.

2

Quote and permit

You approve the scope and price. The permit request goes to the Town Building Division.

3

Install

New pipe goes in through small access openings, and fixtures are reconnected.

4

Test and patch

The system is pressure tested, inspected and the openings are closed for the drywall crew.

Permits and inspections come from the local authority. The Erie Building Division is at 303-926-2780. Drywall repair after a repipe is usually a separate trade, so plan for it.

What To Expect In The House During A Repipe

Expect water to be off for part of each working day and a few small openings in drywall at fixture locations and along the new pipe runs. The plumber lays out a schedule before starting and tells you when each bathroom or the kitchen will be without water.

Plan for dust and noise, move items away from walls the crew will open and keep pets in a closed room. Drywall repair and painting are usually done by a separate trade after the plumbing passes inspection, so schedule them ahead.

Water Quality After A Repipe

New pipe can release a little debris at first. The plumber flushes the system, and you should run each tap for a few minutes before drinking. If you want to test your tap water or add a filter afterward, see water softener and filtration.

Pipe material is regulated. Plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) list the pipe and fitting types approved for potable water, and local authorities adopt and amend that list. Ask the plumber which approved material is going into your walls and why.

Repipe Or Repair?

A single leak in a sound system is a repair. Multiple leaks, widespread corrosion or a known failure-prone pipe are reasons to repipe. The plumber can find each leak and tell you whether the system is still worth patching.
