When A Hidden Leak Becomes An Emergency

Leaks hide in places you rarely look. Supply lines inside walls, pipes under a slab, irrigation lines buried in the yard and fittings behind a washing machine can all drip for weeks. Water often travels before it shows, so the stain is not always near the source.

Signs of a hidden leak

  • The water bill jumps without a change in use
  • The water meter dial moves while every fixture is off
  • Warm spots on a floor, which can mean a hot water line leak
  • Musty smell, peeling paint or bubbling drywall
  • The sound of running water in a quiet house

How We Find A Leak Without Demolition

1

Meter test

We confirm there is a leak by checking whether the meter moves with everything off.

2

Isolate the section

Shutoff valves split the system into zones so we know whether the leak is on the supply side, in a branch or under the yard.

3

Listening equipment, thermal imaging or pressure testing locates the spot within a small area.

4

Report and repair plan

You see the location, the cause and the repair options before any wall or floor is opened.

Erie Water Meters And Who Owns The Pipe

The Town of Erie owns the service line from the main through the meter pit and five feet past the meter. The rest of the line to your house belongs to you. Knowing which side the leak is on tells you who repairs it.

The Town reports that more than 60 percent of its meters are smart meters, with EyeOnWater or WaterScope leak alerts by email or text. If you got an alert, call us with the date and the reading. After a repair, ask Utility Billing at 303-926-2700 about the Town's leak adjustment form.

Leak Types We Handle

Slab leaks, supply line leaks, irrigation leaks and drain leaks each call for a different method. A slab leak under concrete often means rerouting the line overhead instead of breaking the floor. A yard leak between the meter and the house may be a water service line repair.

When the leak is a failed pipe that has already burst, go to burst pipe repair. When the pipes in the whole house are failing, repiping solves the cause instead of the symptom.

A note on pressure. Plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) call for a pressure reducing valve when static pressure exceeds 80 psi, and high pressure makes small leaks worse. The plumber reads the pressure at a hose bib during the visit and tells you if the regulator needs attention.

After The Leak Is Found

Water damage grows quickly. Dry wet drywall and carpet within a day or two, open cabinet doors for airflow and photograph everything before cleanup for insurance. If a leak ran for weeks, ask a restoration company to check for mold.
