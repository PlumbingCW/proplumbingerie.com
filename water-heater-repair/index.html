When A Leaking Water Heater Is An Emergency

Water around the base of a tank can come from a loose fitting, a dripping relief valve or a rusted-through tank. The first two are repairs. The third is a replacement, and the difference is visible once the plumber traces where the water actually starts.

No hot water is a separate question. On an electric unit the cause is often a failed heating element or thermostat. On a gas unit it is usually a pilot, a thermocouple or a gas control valve. Each is a defined repair with a known part.

Call the emergency line if you see any of these

  • Water pooling around the base of the tank
  • The temperature and pressure relief valve discharging steadily
  • A rotten egg smell near a gas unit (leave the house and call your gas utility first)
  • Rust-colored hot water from every hot tap
  • Rumbling or popping from the tank during heating

Gas, Electric Or Hybrid: What Fails On Each

Common water heater failures by type

Electric tankHeating element, thermostat, tripped high-limit switchReplace the element or thermostat and test the circuit
Hybrid heat pumpCondensate drain, fan, sensors, element backupClear the drain, replace the failed part, check settings
Any tankFailed anode rod, leaking fitting, worn relief valveReplace the part. A leaking tank wall means replacement

Hybrid heat pump water heaters are covered here too. They have more parts than a standard electric tank, so the plumber checks the condensate drain and the settings before replacing anything.

Repair Or Replace: How We Decide

Tank age matters more than any single symptom. Most tank-style heaters last roughly eight to twelve years, and a unit near that range with a new leak is a poor candidate for repair. A younger unit with a failed element is usually worth fixing, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

If the plumber recommends replacement, the next step is water heater replacement, and a switch to a tankless water heater is an option when the layout allows it.

A relief valve is a code item, not an accessory. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires a temperature and pressure relief valve on storage water heaters, with a discharge pipe that ends where escaping hot water will not hurt anyone. Never cap or plug that valve, and have a plumber find out why it is opening.

Local Notes For Erie Homeowners

Many Erie homes built during the town's rapid growth have builder-grade tanks now reaching the age where leaks and failed elements appear. A yearly sediment flush and a check of the anode rod extend tank life, and the plumber can do both during a repair visit.

A December 2025 bulletin from the Colorado State Plumbing Board says water heater installs and replacements require a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor, with permits and inspections from the local authority. Ask any plumber you hire to confirm that before work starts.

If a leaking tank has damaged a basement, see leak detection for hidden moisture and sump pump service for water that keeps returning.
