Water Heater Emergency: Signs You Need A New Tank, Not A Repair

A leak from the tank wall cannot be patched. Once rust has worked through the steel, water finds its way out wherever the lining has failed. Rusty hot water, repeated element failures and a tank over a decade old all point the same way.

Replacement is usually the answer when

  • Water seeps from the tank body or the seams, not from a fitting
  • The tank is more than ten to twelve years old and has started to fail
  • Hot water is rust-colored at every tap
  • Repairs have repeated within a year
  • The unit is undersized after a remodel or a growing household

Size matters as much as type. A tank that is too small runs out mid-shower and one that is too large wastes energy. The plumber matches the unit to the number of bathrooms, the peak morning demand and the fuel already in the house.

What Replacement Involves

1

Drain and disconnect

The old tank is drained, the supply lines and gas or power are disconnected and the unit is moved out.

2

Set the new unit

The new heater is placed on a drain pan where code calls for one, with a drain line to a safe location.

3

Connect and vent

Water, gas or electric and venting are connected to current code. A thermal expansion tank is added where the system needs one.

4

Test and walk through

We check for leaks, confirm temperature, test the relief valve and show you the shutoffs.

Permits, Licensing And Local Rules In Erie

The Colorado State Plumbing Board bulletin dated December 4, 2025 states that water heater installs and replacements require a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor, with permits and inspections from the local authority. You can check a plumber's license on the board's licensee lookup.

In Erie, the Town's Building Division handles permits and inspections at 303-926-2780, with applications through its online permit portal. A replacement done without a permit can create problems at the next home sale or an insurance claim. After-hours failures are scheduled through the 24/7 line, with same-day replacement when the unit and the schedule allow.

Several code items travel with every new tank. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, a drain pan where a leak could damage the building and thermal expansion control on closed plumbing systems. The plumber checks each one so the inspection goes smoothly.

After The New Heater Is In

Set the temperature to 120 degrees unless a household member needs it hotter, which lowers scald risk and energy use. Flush the tank once a year and have the anode rod checked every few years. Those two habits stretch the life of the next heater well beyond the first one.

If the old unit leaked, check the floor and wall behind it for moisture. Our leak detection service finds damp spots that are not yet visible. A leaking tank in a finished basement is also a reason to look at sump pump condition.

For a repair that might still save the tank, start with water heater repair. For a new unit in a space that never had one, see water heater installation.
