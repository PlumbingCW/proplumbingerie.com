Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Erie, CO

A water service line is the buried pipe that brings water from the meter to your house. When it leaks or breaks, you may see a wet yard, a drop in pressure or a meter that never stops. Our line is open 24/7 for Erie. While you wait, close the main shutoff inside the house.

The Town of Erie owns part of the service line, so the first step is finding where the break is and who is responsible. Leak detection answers the first question.

Water Line Emergency: Who Owns Which Part Of The Line

The Town of Erie says it owns the service line from the main through the meter pit, plus five feet past the meter. The homeowner owns the remainder. If the leak sits on the Town side, the utility handles it. If it is on yours, you hire a plumber.

The Town also reports that it has no town-owned lead service lines, and Colorado banned lead pipe in 1988. The private-side material depends on the age and builder, so the plumber identifies what you have.

Signs of a service line problem

  • A wet or sunken spot in the yard between the meter and the house
  • Sudden drop in water pressure throughout the house
  • The meter keeps moving with all fixtures off
  • Water bill jumps with no change in use
  • Muddy or discolored water after repair work nearby

Materials And Pressure

Modern service lines use copper, PEX or HDPE rated for buried use. Copper has a long record, while plastic resists corrosion and handles ground movement well. Erie's growing neighborhoods have newer lines, and Old Town homes may have older steel or copper.

A pressure reducing valve near the entry point protects the house from high supply pressure. If your pressure is high, the plumber checks the valve while the line is open.

Pressure Reducing Valves And Shutoffs

Many homes have a pressure reducing valve where the water line enters. It holds house pressure below the level that stresses pipes, fixtures and the water heater. Static pressure above about 80 psi is a common upper limit in plumbing codes, and a gauge on a hose bib shows where yours sits.

While the service line is open, it is the cheapest time to add or replace the main shutoff valve and the pressure regulator. A shutoff that turns freely saves you in the next emergency.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and a line that is flooding the yard is handled through the 24/7 line.

A pressure reducing valve is a code item. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for one when static pressure exceeds 80 psi, and a failed valve can overstress a new service line. According to the Town of Erie, the utility owns the line through the meter pit, so confirm the boundary before you dig.
