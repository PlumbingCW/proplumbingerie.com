When A Backed-Up Line Is An Emergency Jetting Call

A cable cuts a channel through a clog. Water finds the channel and drains for a while, then the remaining buildup closes up again. Hydro jetting removes the buildup from the pipe wall, so the line returns closer to its original diameter.

That makes jetting the better fit for kitchen drain lines, restaurant waste lines and main lines that have slowed more than once. It is also useful before a pipe lining or camera inspection to get a clean view.

Jetting is worth considering when

  • A cable cleared the clog but it came back within weeks
  • The line is slow, not blocked, and the pipe is full of grease
  • Roots have entered at joints and need to be cut back
  • A restaurant or shop line carries kitchen waste
  • Scale has narrowed an older iron or clay line

How A Jetting Visit Works

1

Camera first

We look at the pipe before jetting to confirm it can take the pressure. Cracked, collapsed or offset pipe is not a jetting candidate.

2

Access the line

The hose enters through a cleanout. If none exists, the plumber discusses options such as pulling a toilet.

3

Jet the line

A nozzle with forward and rear jets pulls the hose through while scouring the walls.

4

Check the result

A second camera pass confirms the pipe is clear and shows what is left to repair.

Safety Limits We Respect

High water pressure can damage fragile pipe. Old clay, brittle cast iron and pipe with cracks or offsets can break under a jet. That is why the camera comes first and why we may recommend cable cleaning or repair instead.

Jetting also pushes loosened debris downstream, so the plumber clears from the cleanout toward the city main and checks the line afterward. In older Old Town homes with mixed pipe materials, that sequence matters.

Access to the line is a code matter too. Plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) require cleanouts at defined points in a drain system, and that is what lets a jetter hose enter without pulling a toilet. A home with no accessible cleanout will need one added before jetting is practical.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Stop using water and do not flush until the line is clear
  • Clear the area around the cleanout or the drain that will be used
  • Tell the dispatcher about any past camera inspections or repairs
  • Keep children and pets out of any area where wastewater has surfaced
  • Photograph standing water for your insurer before cleanup

Homes And Businesses That Use Jetting Most

Kitchens and restaurants send grease to the drain, where it cools and hardens. A commercial plumbing account often schedules jetting on a regular cycle. Homes with a basement bathroom installed after construction may also see slow lines that jetting clears.

For a one-time clog, drain cleaning is usually enough. For a collapsed pipe or a line that keeps failing, sewer line cleaning and repair are the next paths. We also serve the Lafayette side of the county with the same process on the Lafayette emergency page.
