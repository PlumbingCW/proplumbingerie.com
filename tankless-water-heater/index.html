Tankless Water Heater in Erie, CO

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to leak or run out. In Erie, the details that matter are incoming water temperature in winter, gas supply and venting. Getting those right decides whether the unit delivers steady hot water.

What A Tankless Water Heater Does Differently

A tank heater keeps a pool of hot water ready and reheats it as it cools. A tankless unit fires a burner or electric element only when a tap opens. That removes standby heat loss and the risk of a flooding tank, but it also puts the full demand on the unit at once.

Flow rate and temperature rise are the two numbers that decide if it works. Winter incoming water in Colorado is much colder than summer water, so a unit that handles a shower in July may struggle in January if it is undersized.

Gas Or Electric Tankless

Gas tankless units give higher flow but need a gas line sized for the burner and a sealed vent to the outside. Condensing models also need a drain for acidic condensate, usually through a neutralizer. Without those, the unit fails inspection or corrodes early.

Electric tankless units avoid venting but draw a large amount of current, often more than a typical panel has to spare. The plumber and an electrician decide if the panel can carry the load. Many homes need an upgrade for a whole-house electric unit.

Descaling And Repairs

Mineral scale builds on the heat exchanger and cuts flow, raises temperature swings and triggers error codes. Flushing with a descaling solution through the service valves clears it. We do not quote a local hardness number because it varies, so test your tap water or ask the Town of Erie utility.

Common repairs include igniters, flame sensors, flow sensors and condensate drains. A softener or filter ahead of the unit reduces scale, and our water softener and filtration page explains the options.

Venting, Gas Lines And Code

Condensing gas units vent through plastic pipe, often out a side wall, and must slope back so condensate drains. Non-condensing units need stainless or specific vent pipe. Mixing vent materials or running them too far voids the install and fails inspection.

A tankless gas unit can draw far more gas than the old tank did. The plumber measures the existing gas line and checks the meter capacity. The Town Building Division at 303-926-2780 handles permits, and the State Plumbing Board's December 2025 bulletin says water heater work requires a licensed plumber working for a registered contractor.

Electric tankless models draw a large current, so the circuit has to meet the National Electrical Code (NEC) and the panel has to have room. An electrician usually sizes and pulls that circuit, while the plumber handles the water connections. Ask who is doing which part before you sign.

Is Tankless Right For Your Home?

Tankless suits households that want continuous hot water, have the gas or electric capacity and plan to stay long enough to recoup the install cost. It is a poor fit when the gas line is far from the unit or when the panel is already full.

If you want to compare, see water heater replacement for tank options and water heater installation for new locations. For a failing tankless unit, repair is often possible.

Tankless work is quoted for ZIP 80516 and for homes in 80026, 80027, 80530 and 80514. Gas supply and venting differ from one Weld or Boulder County address to the next, so the site visit starts at the gas meter and the planned vent path instead of at the unit.
