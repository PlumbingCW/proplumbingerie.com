Plumbing Installation in Erie, CO

Plumbing installation covers new lines, fixtures and drains for a remodel, an addition or a finished basement. The work has to meet code, pass inspection and work every day. We plan the lines before walls close so the finished space works.

Call us at the design stage. A change on paper costs far less than a change after the drywall is up. A new heater belongs in the plan too, as covered under water heater installation.

What Counts As Plumbing Installation

Bathroom additions

New supply, drain and vent lines for a toilet, sink and shower or tub.

Kitchen remodels

Relocating sinks, adding dishwasher lines and gas or water for appliances.

Basement finishing

Rough-in for a bathroom or wet bar, sometimes needing a sewage ejector.

Fixture upgrades

New toilets, faucets, showers and water heaters connected to existing lines.

Planning A Basement Bathroom

A basement bathroom below the sewer line needs special planning. Wastewater cannot flow uphill, so the plumber may install a sewage ejector pit and pump. The pit is sealed and vented, and the pump sends waste up to the main.

Check the slab and the soils too. Colorado's expansive clay soils can heave a slab, and the Colorado Geological Survey publishes a swelling soils guide that builders give to new-home buyers. Ask the plumber how the lines are supported so movement does not strain them.

Working With Other Trades

Plumbing sits in the middle of a remodel. Framers need to know where drains and vents will pass through joists. Electricians need to know where a water heater, disposal or pump will draw power. Drywall and tile crews come after the rough-in inspection.

Share the plans early and ask the plumber to mark fixture locations on the framing. A fifteen-minute walk-through before drywall is hung avoids most surprises.

Questions to ask any plumber before hiring

  • Is the person doing the work licensed, and for which contractor
  • Who pulls the permit and schedules the inspections
  • What is included in the written price and what would change it
  • How are surprises handled once a wall is opened
  • Who handles drywall, tile and paint after the plumbing is done

You can verify a plumber's license on the State Plumbing Board's licensee lookup.

Pressure and venting are code items on any new work. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for a pressure reducing valve when static pressure exceeds 80 psi and sets vent and trap rules for every fixture. The plumber confirms both with the inspector at rough-in.

Backflow And Water Quality

Outdoor spigots, irrigation systems and some fixtures need backflow protection to keep contaminated water from reaching the supply. A hose bib vacuum breaker is a simple version. Irrigation systems and certain commercial uses need an assembly that is tested.

If you are adding a softener or filter at the same time, see water softener and filtration. For a new water heater, see water heater installation. For a problem with an existing drain, start with drain cleaning.
