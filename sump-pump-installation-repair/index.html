Sump Pump Emergency: Why Pumps Fail When You Need Them

A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of the basement and moves groundwater out before it reaches the floor. It sits idle for months, then has to work hard during the wettest days. Switches stick, impellers clog with silt and motors wear out from short-cycling.

Power loss is the other common cause. The same storm that raises the water table often takes out the electricity. A pump with no backup stops exactly when it is needed.

Warning signs of a failing pump

  • The pump runs constantly or cycles on and off every few seconds
  • It hums but does not move water
  • The float rides against the pit wall or sticks above the water
  • Rust, grinding noise or a burning smell from the motor
  • Water in the pit does not drop after the pump starts

A battery backup is the most common upgrade. It runs a second pump during an outage. Test it a few times a year and replace the battery on the maker's schedule.

Keeping A Pump Ready

Pour a bucket of water into the pit each season to confirm the float rises and the pump starts. Clean the intake screen, check that the discharge pipe is clear and watch for ice at the outlet in winter. These two minutes catch most problems.
