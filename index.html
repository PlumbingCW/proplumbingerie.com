What Counts As A Plumbing Emergency In Erie

A plumbing problem is an emergency when water is actively damaging the building, when sewage is coming back into the house or when the home has lost hot or cold water in freezing weather. Waiting a few hours in these cases usually means a bigger repair bill, because water finds drywall, subfloor and basement carpet quickly.

Call the 24/7 line right away for any of these

  • A pipe is spraying or pouring water and the flow does not stop at the fixture valve
  • Sewage or gray water is backing up into a floor drain, tub or toilet
  • A water heater tank is leaking from the base or the pressure relief valve is dumping water
  • The water meter keeps spinning with every fixture in the house turned off
  • There is no water at all, or no hot water on a cold Front Range night
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, wall or basement floor and you cannot see the source

Plumbing Services We Dispatch For In Erie

Drains and sewer lines

A slow kitchen sink or a toilet that gurgles is often the first sign of a deeper clog, so we start with drain cleaning and unclogging and move to hydro jetting when grease or roots keep bringing the clog back. When the main line itself is the problem, a camera visit under sewer inspection shows what is in the pipe before anyone digs, and sewer line repair follows if the pipe is cracked or belled.

Water heaters

A tank that is leaking, rumbling or putting out lukewarm water usually needs water heater repair or, past about ten to fifteen years, water heater replacement. Planned swaps and upgrades are handled under water heater installation, and tankless units get their own flush and service schedule.

Leaks, pipes and fixtures

When the water bill jumps or a ceiling stains, leak detection finds the source without opening every wall. A pipe that has already failed is a burst pipe repair call. We also handle toilets, faucets, garbage disposals, older supply lines that justify repiping and the buried line between the street and your house under water service line repair.

Basements, filtration and commercial work

Erie basements get their own attention through sump pump installation and repair and basement waterproofing. For water quality we install water softeners and filtration, and shops and restaurants can call for commercial plumbing help after hours.

Before The Plumber Arrives

These four steps limit damage in most emergencies. None of them needs tools, and all of them can be done in a few minutes.

1

Shut off the water

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. In most Erie homes it sits where the water line enters the house, often in the basement or a utility closet.

2

Cut power near water

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off the breaker for that area. Stay out of standing water if you are unsure.

3

Clear and contain

Move rugs and boxes, put towels or a bucket under drips and open cabinet doors under the affected sink to let air in.

4

Take photos

Photograph the damage and the source before cleanup. Insurance adjusters ask for them and they help the plumber see what changed.

Why Erie Homes Have The Plumbing Problems They Do

Census counts put Erie at 1,258 residents in 1990, 6,291 in 2000, 18,135 in 2010 and 30,038 in 2020. That means most houses here went up in the last twenty-five years. Builder-grade water heaters from the early growth years are now reaching the age where leaks and failed elements show up, so water heater calls cluster in that age group.

Old Town Erie is a different story. The town was incorporated in 1874, and the first underground coal mine in the area opened in 1876. Older homes in Old Town can still carry original galvanized supply pipe or cast iron drains. A camera look at the drain line before a remodel is cheaper than finding a cracked section after the new bathroom is finished.

Ground conditions matter too. The Colorado Geological Survey publishes a swelling soils guide that builders hand to new-home buyers, because clay that expands when wet can move foundations and buried lines. For a homeowner that is a reason to keep drain slopes clear, watch basement walls and test a sump pump before spring.

Town Water And Who Owns Which Pipe

Erie homes get water from the Town of Erie utility. The town's 2021 water conservation plan, filed with the state, lists Colorado-Big Thompson and Windy Gap supplies delivered through Northern Water, plus local rights on South Boulder Creek and Coal Creek, treated at the Lee Morgan Water Treatment Facility. We do not quote a hardness number here. The Town of Erie publishes water quality information for its customers, so ask the utility for the current report.

The Town of Erie says it owns the service line from the main through the meter pit, plus five feet past the meter. The rest of the line to your house belongs to you. That split matters when a meter keeps running with everything off, because a leak on the home side is a private plumbing repair. The town's utility billing office at 303-926-2700 can explain its leak adjustment process after a repair.

The Rules Plumbers Work Under

According to the State Plumbing Board's December 2025 bulletin, water heater work is governed by Title 12, Article 155 of the Colorado Revised Statutes and by Chapters 5 and 28 of the Colorado Plumbing Code. Model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) set the technical rules for traps, vents, slopes and relief valves, and local authorities adopt and amend them.

Check Any Plumber's License

Colorado licenses plumbers through the State Plumbing Board, part of the Division of Professions and Occupations. You can look up a licensee on the board's plumbing page. A December 2025 board bulletin states that water heater installs and replacements require a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor, with permits and inspections handled by the local authority.
