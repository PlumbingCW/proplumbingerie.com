When A Disposal Problem Is An Emergency

Disposals jam when hard or fibrous waste lodges between the flywheel and the shredder ring. They leak when the sink flange seal, a hose clamp or the housing itself fails. They stop when the motor overheats, burns out or loses power.

Safe Steps While You Wait

Do and do not

  • Switch off the disposal and unplug it or flip its breaker before looking inside
  • Never put your hand into the drain, even when it is off
  • Do not pour chemical cleaner into a jammed disposal
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom only after the unit cools
  • Turn off the water at the angle stops if the cabinet is wet

Repair Or Replace

A jam or reset needs no new unit. A leaking flange can often be resealed. A motor that has burned out or a housing that has cracked is a replacement. Most disposals last roughly eight to fifteen years, so age is part of the decision.

If you are replacing, size the horsepower to your household. A larger motor handles more waste and runs smoother. Disposals with sound insulation are quieter, which matters in an open kitchen.

Dishwasher Connections And Air Gaps

The dishwasher drain hose usually connects to a knockout port on the disposal or to the sink drain through an air gap or a high loop. A loop that sits below the sink flange can let sink water flow back into the dishwasher. The plumber checks that connection during any disposal job.

When a new disposal goes in, the dishwasher port must be opened with a knockout plug removed. Skipping that step makes the dishwasher back up. It is a small detail that causes a big mess when missed.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and after-hours calls go to the same 24/7 line.

The dishwasher connection is a code item. Plumbing codes such as the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) call for the dishwasher drain to rise into a high loop or pass through an air gap so sink water cannot flow back into the machine. The plumber checks that connection when a disposal is installed.

Using A Disposal Well

Run cold water while the disposal works and for a short time after. Cut large scraps small. Keep grease, coffee grounds in bulk, rice, pasta and fibrous vegetable waste out of it. Those items cause most of the clogs.

If the kitchen line clogs after the disposal, the cause is further down the pipe. A hydro jetting service clears grease. For new kitchen work, see plumbing installation, and for faucet problems at the same sink, see faucet repair.
