When A Blocked Main Sewer Line Is An Emergency

The usual causes are grease, wipes, paper, tree roots and children's toys, plus scale in older iron pipe. The blockage sits below every fixture, so the first sign is often a basement drain gurgling or backing up while the rest of the house works.

How to tell it is the main line

  • The lowest drain in the house backs up first
  • Flushing a toilet makes a tub or shower gurgle
  • Several fixtures are slow at the same time
  • Water rises in a floor drain after laundry
  • The cleanout cap shows standing water when opened by a plumber

Storm Drain And Floor Drain Notes

Not every basement floor drain connects to the sanitary sewer. Some tie to a storm or sump line, which behaves differently and should not carry wastewater. The plumber traces where a drain goes before clearing it, because the right cleaning depends on which system it feeds.

Storm water entering a sewer line can overload it during heavy rain. If backups follow storms, tell the plumber, who may look for inflow sources or a failed backwater valve.

What To Do Before The Plumber Arrives

Prepare the work area

  • Stop using water and do not flush until the line is clear
  • Locate the main cleanout, often a capped pipe in the basement, crawlspace or by the foundation
  • Move stored items away from the floor drain and the cleanout
  • Keep children and pets out of any area where sewage has surfaced
  • Photograph any water damage for your insurer before cleanup

Same-day service is available when the schedule allows, and after-hours calls go to the same emergency line. If sewage has reached finished floors, say so on the phone so the plumber can bring protective gear and plan the cleanup.

Where the cleanout sits matters. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for cleanouts at points such as the junction of the building drain and the building sewer, and that access lets the plumber clear the line without removing a fixture. Know where yours is and keep it free of stored items.

Keeping The Main Line Clear

Keep wipes, grease and feminine products out of the toilet and sink. Know where your cleanout is and keep it accessible. If large trees stand near the line, ask for a camera look every few years.
