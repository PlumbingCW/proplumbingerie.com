When A Faucet Failure Is An Emergency

Faucets leak at the spout because of worn cartridges, washers or O-rings. They leak under the sink from supply lines or the base seal. A faucet with low pressure often has a clogged aerator or a partially closed stop valve.

Repair Or Replace

A newer faucet with a failed cartridge is worth repairing. An old faucet with corroded fittings and hard-to-find parts is better replaced. The plumber tells you what can be done on the first visit.

If the shutoff valves under the sink are corroded or stuck, we replace them while the area is open. A shutoff that works saves you from a future emergency.

Outdoor Spigots And Winter

Hose bibs that freeze can split inside the wall and leak when the weather warms. Disconnect hoses before the first freeze and shut off the interior valve if there is one. A frost-free spigot is a good upgrade.

If a spigot leaks inside the wall, treat it as a hidden leak and see leak detection.

Choosing A Replacement Faucet

Start with the sink. Count the holes in the deck and measure the spacing, because a three-hole sink and a single-hole sink take different faucets. Check the clearance behind the faucet if the sink sits against a backsplash, since a tall gooseneck spout may not swing.

Look at the valve inside, not the finish. A ceramic disc or solid cartridge valve lasts longer than a washer-type valve, and metal supply lines last longer than thin plastic ones. A faucet with replaceable parts is easier to repair in ten years.

Aerators And Low Pressure

A faucet that suddenly sprays sideways or runs weakly often has a clogged aerator, the small screen at the tip of the spout. Unscrew it, rinse out any grit and put it back. If the flow stays low at every faucet in the house, the cause is likely a partly closed valve, a pressure regulator or a problem upstream.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and a faucet that will not shut off is an after-hours call we take on the 24/7 line.

Outdoor faucets carry a code requirement as well. Plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) call for backflow protection on hose connections, which is why most hose bibs have a vacuum breaker. Replace one that leaks when the faucet is off, and do not remove it to stop a drip.

Installing A New Faucet

A new faucet needs a matching hole count, the right deck thickness and clear access to the supply lines. We install kitchen, bath and utility faucets and connect them to the shutoffs. Old valves are replaced if they are worn.
