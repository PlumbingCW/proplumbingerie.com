Commercial Plumbing Emergencies We Handle

Drain backups

Floor drains, restroom lines and kitchen lines back up under heavy use.

Leaks and bursts

Water heaters

Commercial hot water demand is larger. A failed unit stops dishwashing and cleaning.

Fixture failures

Flush valves, urinals and faucets see heavy daily use.

Each type of business has its own pattern, with restaurants fighting grease and offices seeing leaks in ceilings where lines run overhead. Retail shops mostly suffer from restroom fixtures. We ask about the business first so we can bring the right tools.

Grease Traps And Kitchen Lines

A grease trap or interceptor catches fats before they enter the sewer. When it is full or poorly maintained, grease reaches the line and hardens. Regular pumping is the first defense, and hydro jetting clears what gets past it.

Kitchens should keep scraps and grease out of sinks. Training staff costs less than a weekend backup. We can recommend a cleaning cycle after seeing the pipe.

Backflow Protection And Testing

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from flowing back into the public supply. Many commercial properties, irrigation systems and fire lines need one, and the device often needs periodic testing. Ask the Town of Erie utility what your property requires.

Preventive Plumbing For Shops, Restaurants And Offices

Most commercial plumbing emergencies have a warning sign. A floor drain that drains slowly after closing, a restroom fixture that runs on, or a faint wet smell near a mop sink usually appears weeks before the failure. Staff who know to report these things early save the business a bad day.

A short monthly walk-through

  • Check floor drains and mop sinks for slow draining or odor
  • Run each restroom fixture and listen for leaks or constant refill
  • Look under sinks and around the water heater for moisture
  • Confirm the main shutoff and area valves turn freely and are labeled
  • Keep a list of who on staff knows where each valve is

After-hours and same-day service are available through the 24/7 line, so you do not have to wait for a Monday call-back after a weekend failure.

Commercial kitchens fall under stricter rules. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for grease interceptors where kitchen waste could clog the sewer and for backflow protection where contaminated water could reach the supply. Local authorities adopt and amend those rules, so ask the Town of Erie Building Division which edition applies.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the water to the affected area and photograph the damage
  • Move inventory, records and electronics off the floor
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet or sewage-affected areas
  • Switch off power near standing water only if you can do so safely
  • Note which fixtures and drains are affected so the dispatcher can plan tools
